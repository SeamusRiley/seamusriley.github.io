Too often I hear statements to the effect of "He's not a bad guy, he's just [insert horrible failing]"

Ethics is a science, not a religion. "Good" and "Bad" are not concepts for how well we serve the interests of the community nor how favorably we will be judged at the pearly gates. Rather being bad just means not functioning that well as an organism. It's not all that "bad" to be a bad person. In fact many of my best friends are terrible people, and they nonetheless have some great redeeming qualities. What makes a person good or bad is how well the organ that is their brain is honest with itself and others, how well it follows through, how well it focuses and executes, and how faithfully it interprets reality. People engage in a lot of counterproductive shame around their bad habits, because they are seeking comfort or approval from a higher power through self-pity, but when you take a spiritually secular look at behavior, shame is counterproductive, and is only a way to be an even worse person. Even less honest. Even less effectual. Bad people have a tendency to slingshot between binge and self-pity, and the only way they rescue themselves from the self-pity is with a shot of unearned confidence that says "I'm a good person, after all, I'm nice to all my friends" and then catapults them into the next binge. No, you did not become a good person, and to a large extent you never will. ADHD people are also bad people. They're bad a being people. That doesn't make them mean or worthless, and they shouldn't take it personally that they're bad at managing their own schedule any more than I take it personally that I can't shoot hoops like Steph Curry. Steph Curry is a very good basketball player. People who can't honor commitments are very bad people. Understanding that, and understanding that it's not so "bad" to be a bad person, is the first step to becoming a slightly less bad person.
