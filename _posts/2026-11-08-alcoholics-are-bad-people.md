an essay about morality entitle "Alcoholics are Bad People" that brings ethics back to being a science and away from being a religion. The article should talk about how it's not all that "bad" to be a bad person, and that in fact in fact many of my best friends are terrible people, and they nonetheless have some great redeeming qualities. Categorically though, what makes a person good or bad is how well they are honest with themselves and others, how well they follow through, how well they focus and execute. People engage in a lot of counterproductive shame around their bad habits, because they are seeking comfort or approval from a higher power through self-pity, but when you take a spiritually secular look at behavior, shame is counterproductive, and is only a way to be an even worse person. Even less honest. Even less effectual. Bad people have a tendency to slingshot between binge and self-pity, and the only way they rescue themselves from the self-pity is with a shot of unearned confidence that says "I'm a good person, after all, I'm nice to all my friends" and then catapults them into the next binge. No, you did not become a good person, and to a large extent you never will. ADHD people are also bad people. They're bad a being people. That doesn't make them mean or worthless, and they shouldn't take it personally that they're bad at managing their own schedule any more than I take it personally that I can't shoot hoops like Steph Curry. Steph Curry is a very good basketball player. People who can't honor commitments are very bad people. Understanding that, and understanding that it's not so bad to be a bad person, is the first step to becoming a slightly less bad person.
