Sure. I like a lot of what I consider to be subversively anti-socialist policies. They border on the concept of "malicious compliance". Another one is to cut social security benefits for high earners. Obviously the capitalist ideal would be to not have income tax or social security at all, but if we have to have these things, I think it is better that we be honest about what these programs are. They are theft from the rich to pay for the poor. Social Security was very slimily billed as something to everyone's benefit. "No see, we all pay into it and we all take out, so it's fair." No, it is not fair, and anyone who can do math knows it. Creating an administrative burden where Elon Musk pays into Social Security so that Jeff Bezos can take his Social Security (a rounding error) out all so that the common man can feel justified in looting them both is ridiculous. Straight expropriation is the most preferable policy in the realm of welfare because people are most capable of seeing it for exactly what it is and voting for the minimum amount of it they can stomach.

The typical objection to such "subversive" policies as I prefer is that they are not "malicious compliance" but in fact "foolish concession". And that if we are going to have social programs, we should make those programs be as fair to the rich as possible. I view this as a very similar category error to people who say we have to restrict immigration until the welfare state is abolished: sacrificing a higher value (the immigration) for the sake of a lower value (welfare state solvency). We should want lots of immigration *despite* the fact that it will overburden social programs, precisely because those social programs should be properly viewed as unsustainable and irresponsible from the jump

Likewise I do not thing that we should make Social Security be maximally moral toward the upper classes. I view that very similar to the scene in Atlas where Tinky Holloway and the other looters sit around a table with Dagny and try to loop her into their debates on what minor percentage of appropriation/restriction is "fair"

What the looters seek is the sanction that their welfare state is fair to all involved. The reality of eliminating income tax for the bottom 50% would be barely different from the reality that already exists.

I think one of the issues is that the welfare state is remarkably more sustainable than it has any right to be. In an enlightened society everyone would just go on strike when they got looted, and the policy would be walked back. Very similar to the situation where they tried to slap Microsoft with anti-trust and Microsoft said that they could break up the company but they'd have to cease service for 10 days, and the government backed down because they knew that a 10 day microsoft service outage would be disastrous for the country. I think there will always be some level of welfare statism in my lifetime purely because a large portion of the rich like it that way, but I believe that the "loot/strike equilibrium" let's call it falls much lower when you have honest wealth redistribution.

Another way to think about it is to consider it in reverse. Suppose I asked you to pay me 100 dollars a month because you have more money than I, and I have less. You would say no, but what if my counter-offer was that I would pay you 10 dollars a month in return to make it more fair. Would you then agree to (effectively) be paying me 90 dollars a month for nothing?

That is essentially the con that Americans have fallen for with many social programs
