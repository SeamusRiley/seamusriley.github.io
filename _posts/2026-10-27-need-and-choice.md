## My stark, unforgiving, and probably misintegrated philosophy of love.

As of today, I have chosen to spend most of my life alone. My romantic relationships have burned brightly and briefly, and every time one ended, it was me who ended it. I saw cracks in the foundation that they perhaps did not. Perhaps there was no way they could have seen them. Sometimes people would say I'm asexual—void of desire. I'm not. 24/7/365 intimacy stays on my mind. I have every bit of my father's libido, but unlike him I am a perfectionist in love. In every other area of my life I have lost my need of perfectionism and embraced an ethos of steady progress, but not in love. In love, man's highest reflection of self, I practice a brutalist ethic of purposefulness over pragmatism, choice over need, and radical self-acceptance.

### What is acceptance really?

Acceptance is the final stage of awareness and the reward of honesty. Acceptance has to be constantly kept separate from resignation, which is counterfeit acceptance. Resignation can be the first step in acceptance, but more often it is a phony attempt to make oneself comfortable with a convenient lie. "I'm going to be alone forever." is a classic morose statement uttered by a teenager going through his first break-up. No matter how he might attempt to pawn the sentiment off as mature acceptance, it is immature resignation. Acceptance by contrast is embodied rather than declared. Someone who has truly accepted his destiny would not feel the need to reiterate it to himself and others, consciously or unconsciously seeking a second-opinion on reality. A perfect example of this relationship and the best scene in Project Hail Mary occurs when space castaway Ryland Grace grandstands to his alien companion Rocky that he has accepted the fact he will die in space due to lack of a fuel source. Rocky realizes that he has enough fuel to spare for Ryland that he need not die in space, and he volunteers it. Ryland is overcome with gratitude and relief.
